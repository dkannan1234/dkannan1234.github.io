Urban Studies Research Technology

Nobody Moves: The Impact of Rideshare Apps on Bengaluru's Urban Landscape

University of Pennsylvania · Urban Studies Thesis · Fall 2025

Bengaluru is one of the most congested cities in the world. There has been a recent surge in rideshare services utilized by affluent residents. This paper will analyze how rideshare platforms affect Bengaluru's transportation infrastructure, mobility inequalities, and increasing platform capitalism. Utilizing semi-structured interviews conducted in-person in August 2025 with Bengaluru drivers, residents, platform owners, and experts, this paper analyzes over forty perspectives to analyze impacts of rideshare platforms. The interviews were conducted in auto-rickshaws, rideshare cars, and public buses, metros, and parks for thirty minutes to an hour, in English, Kannada, and Tamil. The results indicated that rideshare platforms have become a daily solution for affluent residents, making them less likely to use public transit. They also indicated that they create a wider gap in transportation experiences based on class. Additionally, rideshare apps have a monopoly on private rides, which creates driver labor precarity. This paper concludes that rideshare platforms are ultimately a setback for sustainable transportation and social equality in Bengaluru. As Bengaluru implements the new rapid metro system, it is important to consider how rideshare platforms may affect ridership.

Penn Thillana Carnegie Hall Performance

Captain · Penn Thillana · 2025

As the captain of the team, I helped the team achieve this opportunity as one of Penn's 6 performing arts team afforded this chance, and helped with choreography, formations, and rehearsal logistics. London Disconnected: A Spatial Perspective on Broadband Access and Disparity

Exchange Student · University College London · 2024

As an exchange student at University College London's Barlett School of Planning, I conducted a GIS Analysis using data from UK's Consumer Data Research Centre using ArcGIS to understand the relations between broadband Internet access, class, and the Index of Multiple Deprivations. This was a final report for a course on GIS, and my findings stated that lower income areas are less served by broadband access, despite having similarly low need as higher income neighborhoods.
